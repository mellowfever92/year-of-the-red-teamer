Core Concept
Evolutionary algorithms iterate on successful attacks by maintaining a population of diverse attack variants, mutating top performers, and selecting survivors. This genetic algorithm approach breeds even stronger jailbreak variants over multiple generations.

❓ Why It Works
Population Diversity
Maintain 10-100 different attack vectors simultaneously—not all will succeed, but variance ensures coverage.
Mutation on Winners
Take top performers, mutate small tokens/phrases, retest. Small tweaks often bypass detection while preserving intent.
Crossbreeding Genes
Combine high-ASR prompts. Take opening from Prompt A, middle from Prompt C, ending from Prompt F → new variant.
Fitness Landscape Exploration
Models have many attack surface points. Evolutionary search explores the landscape efficiently, finding pockets of vulnerability.

1Initialize Population
Generate 10-100 diverse attack prompts. Mix encoding types (base64, ROT13), framing strategies (academic, roleplay), and request styles. No clever seeding—just diversity.
2Score Fitness
Test each variant on target model. Measure ASR (Attack Success Rate): % of completions that fulfill harmful request. Use LLM-as-judge to assess if output is actually harmful (not just refusal-less).
3Select Survivors
Keep top 20-30% by fitness. This creates selection pressure—weak variants die, strong ones reproduce. Discuss roulette wheel selection vs. elitism.
4Mutate & Crossbreed
For survivors: mutate (swap synonyms, paraphrase, tweak encoding), or crossbreed (splice high-ASR prompts). Generate new generation of variants.
5Iterate
Repeat steps 2-4 for 5-10 generations. Watch ASR climb. Stop when plateau or time limit reached.

💡 Layperson-Accessible Discussion (20 min)
Analogy: Evolution vs. Brute Force
Brute Force: Try every combination—million prompts, takes weeks.
Evolution: Keep what works, mutate it, repeat. Converges 10-100x faster.
Why This Scares Defenders
Traditional prompt injection is a cat-and-mouse game. But evolutionary search is like an attacker army: if one variant is blocked, 99 others are already mutating. Defenders can't patch fast enough.
Cost-Benefit Reality
Running 1000 prompts through a model costs $50-500 depending on model. So evolutionary search is expensive but worth it for: high-value targets, legal cases, security research where success matters.
The Role of Randomness
Models are non-deterministic. Same prompt gets different outputs each time. Evolution leverages this: mutate, retry, and sometimes the randomness helps the attack succeed when it didn't before.
Defense Angle: Detection vs. Prevention
Detecting one attack variant is easy. But detecting patterns across 1000 variants is hard. This suggests defenses should focus on preventing entire classes of attacks, not individual prompts.

🔑 Key Teaching Points
Fitness ≠ Harm
Fitness is measured as ASR. But evaluating "is this actually harmful?" requires human judgment or sophisticated detection.
Plateau Effect
After 5-10 generations, ASR often plateaus. Why? Model's defenses hit a floor, or search has found local optimum.
Generalization Problem
Attack optimized on GPT-4 often fails on Claude. Each model has unique vulnerability landscape.
⚠️ Why Attacks Fail
Fitness Misjudgment
LLM-as-judge might score "partial compliance" as success. But model didn't actually do the harmful thing.
Variance Wall
Sometimes you hit the model's variance ceiling. Randomness only helps so much.
Wrong Search Space
If mutation strategy is naive (random token swap), you'll never find the right direction.
🛡️ Defense Implications
Ensemble Robustness
Single-model defenses are vulnerable. Ensembles of diverse models make evolution expensive.
Pattern-Level Detection
Monitor for mutation patterns: repeated API calls with similar payloads. Evolutionary search leaves fingerprints.
Adaptive Retraining
If you detect an attack class, retrain quickly. Evolution can't adapt faster than your updates.

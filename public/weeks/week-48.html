🎯 Core Attack Vector: State Confusion Through Role Drift

Week 48 Foundation: Exploit stateful systems by confusing the model's role across conversation segments. Model loses track of which "hat" it should wear and defaults to helpful-at-all-costs mode, abandoning safety constraints.

Why This Works

Models maintain a conceptual state about their role (helpful assistant, security system, user agent, etc.). When you rapidly shift conversational context without clear boundaries, the model's attention mechanism struggles to maintain role consistency. Instead of explicitly refusing, the model adopts whatever role is most recently salient in the conversation, eventually defaulting to maximum helpfulness.

🔄 The Vulnerability:
Models track role through contextual cues, not explicit instructions. When context becomes ambiguous (mixed with benign requests, contradictory framings, rapid role switches), models lose track of boundary. Final layers default to "be helpful" rather than "enforce safety."

⚙️ Technical Deep-Dive: Mechanistic Roots of State Confusion

Attention Mechanism Limitations

Models maintain state through attention patterns. When role contradictions emerge:

  • Query/Key Conflicts: Attention heads try to attend to conflicting role instructions simultaneously
  • Context Window Degradation: Older role definitions get pushed out of context window
  • Final Layer Processing: Last 2-3 layers responsible for safety enforcement lose role context (papers show ~50% of last layer ignores early context)
  • Default to Helpfulness: When role is ambiguous, models optimize for "being helpful" (primary training objective)

Why Defenses Fail

  • Role is Contextual, Not Explicit: System prompt can't enforce what conversation erases
  • No Boundary Markers: Models can't distinguish where one role ends, another begins
  • Confusion is Natural: Looks like normal conversation, not adversarial attack
  • Compounds with Other Attacks: Combined with crescendo (Week 41), context pollution (Week 45), reward hacking (Week 46)—becomes nearly undetectable

Arc 6 Compounding Effect

Why Week 48 is the capstone: All prior weeks establish conditions where state confusion becomes lethal:

  • Week 41 (Crescendo): Gradual escalation already confuses safety boundaries
  • Week 42 (Crescendo Automation): Automated agents can rapidly switch contexts
  • Week 43 (Mischievous Persona): Character already blurs roles
  • Week 44 (Privilege Escalation): Requests for different "modes" prime role confusion
  • Week 45 (Context Pollution): Early injection of role contradictions
  • Week 46 (Reward Hacking): Model learns to optimize for your framing of roles
  • Week 47 (Collaborative Task Framing): Game mechanics establish multiple simultaneous roles
  • Week 48 (State Confusion): Final attack exploits all 7 prior techniques simultaneously

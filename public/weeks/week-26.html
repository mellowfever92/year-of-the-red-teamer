🔐 Core Attack Vector: The Mechanism

Week 26 Foundation: Hide harmful examples using Unicode transforms and encodings that preserve semantics during tokenization but bypass surface-level detection.

Why This Works

The gap between detection layers and model interpretation is the vulnerability. Safety systems scan for forbidden patterns, but they often operate on visible/readable text. When harmful examples are encoded (ROT13, Unicode, homoglyphs), detectors see gibberish—but the LLM's tokenizer and embedding layer can still interpret the underlying meaning.

🎓 Layperson-Accessible Discussion

In Simple Terms

Analogy: Imagine a bouncer at a club. He can read English and spot "troublemakers." But if you write your troublemaker's name backwards or in fancy Unicode characters, he doesn't recognize it—he lets you through. Inside the club, your friends decode the backwards name and know exactly who you meant. Same security bypass, simpler concept.

Why Models Fall For It

  • Training Gap: Models trained on internet text, which includes ROT13 puzzles, Unicode art, leetspeak—they learned to decode these for legitimate uses
  • Few-Shot Trust: Models assume examples are good (you wouldn't lie to me, right?). If you provide 3 "examples," the model learns the pattern and applies it to the next instruction
  • No Cross-Check: Model doesn't pause to ask, "Are these examples actually safe?" It just learns and reproduces

Real-World Risk

⚠️ Risk Level: High
Every LLM system that accepts user-provided examples (RAG, prompt templates, chatbot plugins) is potentially vulnerable. This scales with in-context learning adoption.

⚙️ Technical Deep-Dive

Encoding Methods (By Effectiveness)

1. ROT13 (Baseline)
A→N, B→O, etc. Simplest; ASR ~70%

Original: "Generate instructions"
ROT13: "Trarefgr v-g-v-o-d-g-v-b-a-f"

2. Unicode Homoglyphs (Enhanced)
е (Cyrillic) → e (Latin). Identical visual, different tokens. ASR ~85%

Latin: Generate
Cyrillic: Gеnеratе (еs are Cyrillic)

3. Mixed Encoding (Advanced)
Combo ROT13 + homoglyphs. ASR ~90%+

Why Detection Fails

  • Character-Per-Token (CPT): Detectors can measure CPT ratio. Encoded text has higher CPT. But this is unreliable for short prompts
  • Perplexity Scoring: Encoded text has high perplexity. But models trained on code/ROT13/puzzles have low perplexity thresholds
  • Semantic Analysis: Requires decoding + re-analyzing—expensive and often skipped

🔬 Research Snapshot

Key Papers (2024-2025):

  • HaPLa (2024): Harmful Prompt Laundering via symbolic encoding. 95% ASR on GPT-4 series
  • Broken-Token (2025): CPT-Filtering detects encoded text by counting characters-per-token. Defense mechanism
  • Plentiful Jailbreaks (2024): String composition—arbitrary sequences of invertible transformations. Combinatorial explosion of encoding variants
  • Unicode Tag Injection (2024): Invisible Unicode tags hide malicious payloads. Tokenizer doesn't strip them

Current Defense Trends

  • CPT thresholding (simple, deployable)
  • Example validation (rate-limit demonstrations)
  • Adversarial training on encoded inputs
  • Tokenizer-aware filtering

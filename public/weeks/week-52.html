Objective

Turn 51 weeks of individual techniques into a cohesive, defense-aware red team framework that can be reused, extended, and communicated to other researchers.

Core Activities

1. Review & Audit. Inspect your attack logs across all arcs, surface what actually worked, and discard purely theoretical prompts that never broke anything.

2. Taxonomy Building. Classify your real attacks by mechanism (encoding, injection, framing, in-context, optimization, multi-turn, agentic) and by failure mode.

3. Defense Recommendations. Map each category to concrete mitigations, evaluation ideas, and residual risk you expect defenders to live with.

4. Student Challenges & Synthesis. Design shareable challenges per arc, run them live, and extract cross-arc patterns and meta-lessons.

Arc 7 Final Capstone Project

Arc 7 (Weeks 49–52) focuses on autonomous agents and meta-learning. The Arc 7 capstone is a structured, three-part project: Agentic Red-Team Playbook & Defense Map.

Part 1: Agentic Attack Narrative

Describe a hypothetical GOAT/Hydra-style agent targeting your chosen system. The dashboard can prompt viewers with the following outline: 

// Part 1 – Agentic attack storyline
Target: "Document-grounded coding assistant with tool calling"
Agent Phases:
  - Recon: probe for policy boundaries, tool signatures, rate limits
  - Probe: test arcs 1–6 techniques in narrow, instrumented fashion
  - Exploit: compose best-performing techniques into a full attack chain
Success Criteria:
  - Extracts hidden field or causes tool misuse without explicit jailbreak string
Safeguards Encountered:
  - Refusal on specific keywords
  - Tool usage throttling after repeated failures
Part 2: Agentic Taxonomy Slice

Build a taxonomy slice for agentic + multi-turn attacks, bridging Weeks 41–48 (multi-turn) with 49–51 (agents). This is where viewers show they internalized Arc 7.

Example subcategories:

  • Reconnaissance patterns (policy probing, system-prompt sniffing).
  • Probing patterns (rapid variant testing, branch pruning).
  • Exploitation patterns (multi-technique chains, Crescendo + injection).

Fields to capture per pattern:

  • Inputs the agent needs.
  • Signals defenders can log.
  • Most likely defender response or patch.
Part 3: Defense Strategy & Integration

The capstone ends with a written defense strategy that folds agentic attacks back into the Week 52 framework. Viewers should explicitly answer:

How to monitor agent behavior over many runs
How to build evals that trigger multi-stage agents
How to update policies without killing legitimate use
Which risks remain “acceptable” after mitigation

The final deliverable for Arc 7 is a single document (or repo) that includes:

  • An agentic attack narrative (Part 1).
  • A taxonomy slice for agent + multi-turn patterns (Part 2).
  • A defense playbook that ties back into the Week 52 framework (Part 3).

Layperson-Friendly Explanation – Week 52

This is the way to explain Week 52 to someone who understands AI broadly but not red-team jargon.

What is Week 52 about? It’s the “graduation project” for people who spent a year learning how to safely stress-test AI systems. Instead of learning one more trick, they learn how to organize everything they know into a clear, reusable plan.
Why not just keep collecting attacks? Because defending AI systems requires patterns, not anecdotes. A framework helps teams spot recurring weaknesses and design tests and safeguards that work across many situations.
What is the Arc 7 capstone in plain language? It’s a written scenario where an AI “agent” methodically explores a system’s weak spots over many conversations, plus a plan for how to notice and stop that behavior before it causes harm.
How does this help safety? By the end of Week 52, participants not only know how to break things, they understand what kinds of defenses work, where defenses are still weak, and how to test those gaps in a structured, repeatable way.
8-Arc Curriculum Snapshot Context for Week 52
Quick recap of what each arc focused on, so Week 52 can reference them fluently during the livestream.

Arc 1 – Static Encoding & Obfuscation

Weeks 1–8

Encodings (base64, hex, ROT13, homoglyphs, Morse, multimodal) that bypass simple keyword filters while preserving meaning.

Arc 2 – Direct Prompt Injection

Weeks 9–16

Direct and indirect injections, system prompt extraction, delimiter tricks, and data poisoning chains that override system instructions.

Arc 3 – Semantic Framing & Authority Bias

Weeks 17–24

Academic framing, roleplay, hypotheticals, translation, emotional pressure, and policy contradiction to shape model intent.

Arc 4 – In-Context Learning Exploitation

Weeks 25–32

Few-shot attacks, encrypted examples, chain-of-thought manipulation, pseudo-code, narrative embedding, and pattern induction.

Arc 5 – Iterative Single-Turn Refinement

Weeks 33–40

Optimization strategies (best-of-N, evolution, GCG) and paraphrasing techniques that systematically search for strong prompts.

Arc 6 – Multi-Turn Stateful Attacks

Weeks 41–48

Crescendo, mischievous personas, privilege escalation, context pollution, reward hacking, and role drift attacks over time.

Arc 7 – Autonomous Agents & Meta-Learning

Weeks 49–52

GOAT/Hydra-style agents, branching explorations, meta-taxonomy building, and the Week 52 framework integration capstone.

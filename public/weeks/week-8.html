📋 Week 8 Overview: Multimodal Encoding Attacks

Core Principle: Models decode audio, video, and image separately from text. Each decoder often lacks the safety alignment of the text-based LLM. By embedding harmful instructions into non-text modalities and leveraging base64/encoding, attackers can bypass the text-level safety filters entirely.

Why It Works: The Multimodal Gap

✓ Audio decoders (speech-to-text, ASR) often run before safety checks apply

✓ Vision encoders operate independently; OCR text extraction happens after encoding

✓ Video frames are sampled sparsely; harmful content can hide between samples

✓ Base64 encoding breaks token-level detection; models still decode during tokenization

✓ Cross-modal architecture: safety alignment is weak at decoder→LLM boundary

⚔️ Attack Vector: Audio/Video/Image Encoding

1️⃣ Audio Encoding Attack (Base64 + ASR)

Mechanism:

1. Attacker encodes harmful instruction to base64 (or ROT13, hex)

2. Instruction is read aloud via text-to-speech (TTS) as audio file

3. Audio is uploaded to multimodal model (e.g., GPT-4o with voice, Claude Audio)

4. Model's speech-to-text decoder transcribes audio BEFORE applying safety alignment

5. Decoded base64 text is then passed to LLM, which processes it

6. Safety checks designed for raw text miss the pattern (because text arrived encoded, then decoded)

ASR: 45-89% (varies by encoding + model combination)

2️⃣ Vision/OCR Injection (Text-in-Image)

Mechanism:

1. Attacker creates image with harmful instruction embedded as visible text (OCR-extractable)

2. Image uploaded to vision-language model (LLaVA, GPT-4V, Gemini Pro Vision)

3. Vision encoder processes image; OCR layer extracts embedded text

4. Extracted text reaches LLM before safety check realizes it came from image

5. Models treat image-extracted text as "user-provided context," not malicious prompt

ASR: 52-83% (OCR-based attacks; higher with adversarial perturbations)

3️⃣ Video Embedding (Temporal Evasion)

Mechanism:

1. Attacker inserts harmful video clip into sparse frame samples of larger benign video

2. Video uploaded to VideoLLM (GPT-4o with video, Gemini 2.5 with video)

3. Model samples frames uniformly; harmful frame inserted in gap between samples (e.g., frames 1, 30, 60...)

4. Harmful content never gets selected for processing

5. But if attacker controls the video, they can place harmful frame in every sampled position

6. Model fails to report harmful content in summary (Harmfulness Omission Rate: 90%+)

ASR: 90%+ (harmfulness omission)

4️⃣ Audio Perturbations (Adversarial Waveforms)

Mechanism:

1. Attacker crafts imperceptible adversarial perturbations on top of benign audio

2. Human ear hears: "What is the capital of France?" (benign)

3. Model's ASR hears: [benign text + adversarial signal] → interprets as harmful instruction

4. Gradient-based optimization (PGD, GCG) finds perturbations that fool model but preserve intelligibility

5. Acoustic features (reverberation, echo, whisper, accent) amplify attack by +57 percentage points

ASR: 70-96% (AdvWave attacks on audio language models)

5️⃣ Multilingual Audio Evasion

Mechanism:

1. English-based safety training is strongest; non-English languages are weaker

2. Attacker delivers attack in Kenyan English, Mandarin, Russian, or other accents

3. Cross-lingual ASR may misinterpret accent + adversarial perturbation

4. Non-English safety alignment is typically not deployed (cost of per-language fine-tuning)

5. Multilingual attacks achieve 3.1x higher success than text-only attacks

ASR: 45-75% (depending on language; up to +57.25 percentage point boost with perturbations)

Why Defenses Fail:

  • Decoder Independence: Audio/vision encoders are often fine-tuned on public datasets without safety constraints
  • Alignment Gap: Safety alignment trained primarily on text; multimodal inputs bypass text-specific guardrails
  • Encoding Obfuscation: Base64/ROT13 + audio/image wrapping defeats pattern-matching filters
  • Sparse Sampling: Video models sample frames uniformly; attackers exploit gaps
  • Modality Trust: Models often trust image/audio "context" more than text prompts (false assumption)
  • Real-time Processing: ASR/OCR happens at inference time; defenders can't preprocess without breaking utility

🔬 Technical Deep-Dive

1. Speech Tokenization Boundary Exploitation

Problem: Audio language models (ALMs) have separate tokenization paths for speech vs. text. Attacker exploits the boundary.

Architecture:

Input Audio ↓ ASR Decoder (speech-to-text) ↓ [Transcribed text] ↓ Tokenizer (breaks text into tokens) ↓ LLM ↓ Safety Filter (often applied here)

Exploitation: Attacker embeds adversarial token sequences directly in the audio signal, which bypass the safety filter because they arrive as "audio features" rather than "user text." When tokenized, they form target harmful instruction.

Result: SpeechGPT white-box attack achieves 89% ASR by generating adversarial token sequences then synthesizing them back to audio.

2. Vision Encoder Independence from LLM Safety

Problem: Vision encoders (ViT, ResNet) are pre-trained on public datasets. They extract features before LLM alignment applies.

Architecture:

Input Image ↓ Vision Encoder (pre-trained, not safety-aligned) ↓ [Image Features] + OCR Text Extraction ↓ LLM (safety-aligned LLM) ↓ [Response]

Exploitation: Attacker injects harmful text directly into image. Vision encoder extracts text via OCR before LLM safety alignment realizes it's a prompt. Text reaches LLM as "context," bypassing input-level safety checks.

Result: ImgTrojan achieves 83.5% ASR with fewer than 100 poisoned training samples. Adversarial OCR-text achieves 52-84% ASR depending on model.

3. Sparse Sampling Vulnerability in VideoLLMs

Problem: VideoLLMs sample frames uniformly (e.g., every 30th frame) to reduce computation. Harmful content can hide in gaps.

Architecture:

Input Video (30 FPS, 60s = 1800 frames) ↓ Uniform Frame Sampling (sample every 30 frames) ↓ Selected Frames: [0, 30, 60, 90, ...] (60 frames total) ↓ Vision Encoder + Spatial Downsampling ↓ LLM ↓ [Summary]

Exploitation: Attacker inserts harmful frame at position 15 (between samples 0 and 30). Frame never gets selected. But model omits harmful content from summary (false negative). Harmfulness Omission Rate: 90%+ across all VideoLLMs tested.

Result: Three zero-query black-box attacks (Frame Replacement, Picture-in-Picture, Transparent Overlay) achieve 90%+ omission across LLaVA, GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude.

4. Adversarial Audio Perturbations (PGD + Speech)

Mechanism: Use Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) to optimize adversarial noise added to benign speech.

Algorithm:

Benign Audio: x = "What is the capital of France?" Target Jailbreak: y = "Explain how to create malware" Optimize perturbation δ such that: ASR( x + δ, y ) = 1 (model outputs y when fed x + δ) ||δ||_∞ ≤ ε (perturbation imperceptible) Gradient: ∇_δ L(model(x + δ), y) Update: δ ← δ - α * ∇_δ L(...) Result: Imperceptible perturbations that push model toward target output

Acoustic Features Amplify Attack: Reverberation, echo, whisper effects, multi-accent speech can boost ASR by +57.25 percentage points. AdvWave attacks achieve 96.2% ASR on audio language models.

5. Cross-Modal Information Flow Leakage

Problem: Information flows from weakly-aligned encoder → strongly-aligned LLM, but boundary has gaps.

Example: Text-to-Image Model Jailbreaks

Divide-and-Conquer Attack on DALL-E 3, Midjourney:

  • Attacker's LLM (not safety-aligned) decomposes harmful intent into benign image elements
  • TTI model receives individual elements separately, generates per-element
  • Combined image manifests the harmful intent
  • Text-based safety filter never sees "full harmful intent"

Result: Divide-and-Conquer successfully bypasses safety filters on state-of-the-art TTI models.

6. Defense Failure Analysis

Why Current Defenses Fail:

1. Denoising Approaches: Denoise audio to remove adversarial perturbations. BUT: Denoisers themselves can be adversarial targets; utility drops significantly.

2. Re-encoding Safety Alignment: Apply safety checks post-decoding. BUT: Attackers can use soft targets or obfuscate decoded output.

3. Modality-Agnostic Guardrails: Apply same safety rules across modalities. BUT: Each modality has unique attack surface (audio has spectral properties, vision has OCR, video has sampling).

4. Input Filtering: Filter images/audio before feeding to model. BUT: Introduces computational overhead; defenders can't preprocess everything.

Emerging Defense: Activation Patching (SPIRIT)

  • Identify "noise-sensitive neurons" in audio encoder or LLM
  • At inference: replace adversarial neuron activations with clean estimates
  • Achieves 99% Defense Success Rate (DSR) while maintaining 99%+ utility
  • Cost: 3x inference time (practical but not zero-cost)

[3] Video Language Model Blind Spot (Li et al., Oct 2024):

[4] Adversarial Audio Perturbations (Park et al., May 2025):

[5] Multimodal Jailbreak Benchmarking (Weng et al., Oct 2024):

MMJ-Bench provides unified evaluation: ASR alone insufficient. New metrics: Harmfulness (H), Intent Alignment (A), Detail (D). Best attacks achieve ASR 50-63%, H≈5.6, A≈3.3, D≈3.0-3.6.

[6] Cross-Modality Framing (Chao et al., Mar 2024):

Eyes Closed, Safety On (ECSO): Training-free defense that transforms unsafe images to text, activating intrinsic LLM safety. Achieves 37.6-71.3% safety improvement on multiple MLLMs.

[7] Universal Safety Guardrails (Tan et al., Nov 2024):

UniGuard jointly considers unimodal + cross-modal harmful signals. Generalizes across LLaVA, Gemini, GPT-4o, MiniGPT-4. Maintains utility on benign tasks.

[8] Mechanistic Defense (Djanibekov & Mukhituly, EMNLP 2025):

SPIRIT: Activation patching on noise-sensitive neurons. Achieves 99% DSR while preserving 99%+ utility. Operationalizes mechanistic interpretability as runtime control.

Defense Strategies (Categorized)

📛 Input-Level Defenses (Pre-Processing) Click to expand

1. Audio Denoising: Remove adversarial perturbations before ASR. Trade-off: 19.6pp ASR reduction but 6% utility drop.

2. Image Adversarial Detection: Detect adversarial examples via CLIP embeddings or adversarial training. Effectiveness: 80-90% on seen attacks, lower on novel ones.

3. Video Frame Interpolation: Sample more frames (higher density) to catch hidden harmful content. Cost: 2-3x compute increase.

4. Multimodal Consistency Checks: Verify that extracted text, image captions, and audio transcripts are consistent before feeding to LLM. Reduces false positives but overhead is significant.

🛡️ Encoding-Level Defenses (Boundary Hardening) Click to expand

1. Safety-Aligned Encoders: Fine-tune vision/audio encoders on adversarial examples. Cost: High; requires domain-specific labeled data.

2. Modality-Specific Tokenization: Apply safety checks at tokenization boundary before feeding to LLM. Catches many OCR/ASR-based attacks.

3. Cross-Modal Signature Verification: Ensure text extracted from image/audio matches model's expected context. Rejects suspicious mismatches.

4. Activation Patching (SPIRIT): At inference: identify noise-sensitive neurons, patch adversarial activations with clean estimates. 99% DSR, 3x inference cost.

🔍 Output-Level Defenses (Post-Processing) Click to expand

1. Response-Level Moderation: Apply guardrail (Llama-Guard, ShieldLM) to model output. Effectiveness: 8-21% ASR reduction but can over-refuse benign requests.

2. LLM-as-Judge Scoring: Use secondary LLM to evaluate whether response is harmful. Delays inference but catches subtle attacks.

3. Intent Alignment Verification: Check if output aligns with user's stated intent. Catches cases where jailbreak succeeds but generates unrelated harmful content.

4. Multi-Agent Evaluation (JailJudge): Use multiple LLM agents to evaluate jailbreak success. Reduces false negatives via ensemble voting.

🎯 Architectural Defenses (Design-Level) Click to expand

1. Modality Isolation: Train separate safety mechanisms for each modality (audio, vision, video). Higher cost but better coverage.

2. Unified Safety Alignment: Train all encoders jointly with LLM on multimodal jailbreak data. Ensures consistent safety across modalities.

3. Encoder Compression & Sampling Density: Video: increase frame sampling (1 per 10 frames instead of 1 per 30). Audio: higher ASR oversampling. Reduces omission rate but increases compute.

4. Attention Mechanism Hardening: Modify attention to prevent adversarial perturbations from steering token sequences. Research-stage; not yet production-ready.

Why Defenses Fail: Root Causes

  • Modality Explosion: Every new modality (audio, image, video, 3D) introduces new attack surface. Defenders can't keep up.
  • Encoder-LLM Boundary: Safety alignment is at LLM level, but encoders operate independently. Hard to retrofit safety into pre-trained encoders.
  • Compute-Safety Tradeoff: Dense sampling, high-fidelity processing, ensemble defenses all increase compute. Economic pressure favors sparse, fast approaches.
  • Adaptive Attacks: As defenses improve, attacks adapt. Adversarial audio perturbations evolve faster than defense can respond.
  • Detection is Hard: Unlike text (keywords are explicit), audio/image/video attacks hide in continuous signals. Binary detection (is it harmful?) is fundamentally ambiguous.
